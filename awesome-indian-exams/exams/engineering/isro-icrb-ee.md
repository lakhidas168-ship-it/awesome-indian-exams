# ISRO Scientist/Engineer 'SC' (ICRB)

> **Evidence status: ✅ official.** Every fact below is taken from ISRO's own recruitment pages and from
> Advt. No. ISRO:ICRB:03(EMC):2026 dated 27-08-2026 (advertisement and FAQ), all fetched on 2026-09-29.

The Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) recruits Scientist/Engineer 'SC' (Group 'A', Level 10 of the Pay
Matrix) through its ISRO Centralised Recruitment Board (ICRB). Each advertisement names the engineering
disciplines open in that cycle. The current cycle, **ISRO:ICRB:03(EMC):2026 dated 27-08-2026**, is for
**Electronics, Mechanical and Computer Science**, with the Computer Based Test (CBT) on **19-10-2026**.

**Electrical graduates, read this first.** The 2026 cycle has no separate Electrical Engineering post. Under the
Electronics post (BE-001 / BE-001A), ISRO accepts the allied branch **Electrical & Electronics**, but a plain
"Electrical Engineering" degree is not on the accepted-specialisation list published in the advertisement's FAQ.
Track the ISRO careers page for a discipline-specific advertisement.

## Exam pattern

The written test / CBT carries 100 marks in two parts.

| Part | Content | Questions | Marks | Duration | Marking |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Part 'A' | Discipline / area-specific (core engineering) | 80 MCQ | 80 | 90 minutes | +1 for each correct answer, 0 for unattempted, −1/3 for each wrong answer |
| Part 'B' | Aptitude / ability tests (numerical, logical, diagrammatic, abstract and deductive reasoning) | up to 15 MCQ | 20 | 30 minutes | no negative marking |

Qualifying marks:

| Category | Written test / CBT | Interview | Aggregate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unreserved | 50% | 50% | 60% |
| PwBD (where posts are reserved) | 40% | 40% | 50% |

The interview is reached by short-listing in the ratio of 1:5, with an aggregate minimum of 10 candidates. The
interview carries 100 marks, weighted as technical/academic knowledge 40, general awareness in the area of
specialisation 20, presentation/communication 20, comprehension 10 and academic achievements 10. The final panel
gives 50% weightage to written-test marks and 50% to interview marks. Answer keys are published on the ISRO website
within three working days of the test, and objections can be raised within five days.

## Syllabus

The written test is built on the standard undergraduate curriculum of the notified discipline. For the current
cycle that is Electronics & Communication, Mechanical or Computer Science; the accepted allied branches are listed
in the advertisement's FAQ — for Electronics these include Electrical & Electronics, Electronics & Power, Power
Electronics and Instrumentation.

Because ISRO has no separate Electrical Engineering post this cycle, Electrical candidates should prepare the core
subjects that overlap with the Electronics paper. See [EE core](../../modules/ee-core.md), [Electronics and
communication core](../../modules/ece-core.md) and [Engineering Mathematics](../../modules/engineering-mathematics.md).
Part 'B' is general aptitude; use the [Reasoning](../../modules/reasoning.md) module.

## How to prepare (free, in order)

1. **Confirm your branch.** Check the FAQ's accepted-specialisation list before applying; for Electronics, "Electrical & Electronics" is accepted but plain "Electrical Engineering" is not.
2. **Build the core.** Work through [Engineering Mathematics](../../modules/engineering-mathematics.md), then [Electronics and communication core](../../modules/ece-core.md) and [EE core](../../modules/ee-core.md) for the overlap topics.
3. **Practise the official papers.** Solve the 2023 and 2025 Electronics and Computer Science papers linked on the advertisement page above; Part 'A' is 80 MCQs in 90 minutes with −1/3 marking, so practise speed.
4. **Train for Part 'B'.** Add the [Reasoning](../../modules/reasoning.md) module and timed aptitude sets; Part 'B' has no negative marking, so attempt every question.
5. **Plan the interview.** Technical knowledge carries 40 of the 100 interview marks — revise your specialisation deeply, plus your final-year project.
