# GATE Electrical Engineering (EE)

GATE Electrical Engineering (EE) is a premier national-level examination used for M.Tech/ME/PhD admissions with financial assistance, and serves as a major recruitment filter for Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs) across India.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Conducted by | IISc Bengaluru and seven IITs for NCB-GATE (IIT Madras organises GATE 2027) |
| Exam dates | 6, 7, 13, 14, 20 and 21 February 2027; paper-wise schedule on the official site |
| Who can apply | Candidates in the 3rd or higher year of an undergraduate degree, or who have completed a UGC/AICTE-approved degree, in Engineering/Technology/Architecture/Science/Commerce/Arts/Humanities; no age limit |
| Score validity | 3 years |

## Exam pattern

| Section | Marks |
|---|---:|
| General Aptitude (GA) | 15 |
| Engineering Mathematics | 13 |
| Subject Questions (EE) | 72 |
| **Total** | **100** |

- **Questions:** 65 in total — 10 in General Aptitude and 55 in the EE paper.
- **Duration:** 3 hours (180 minutes).
- **Question types:** MCQ (Multiple Choice Question), MSQ (Multiple Select Question), NAT (Numerical Answer Type). Questions carry 1 or 2 marks.
- **Negative marking:** Only for MCQs. 1/3 mark deducted for a wrong 1-mark MCQ, 2/3 mark for a wrong 2-mark MCQ. No negative marking for MSQ or NAT, and no partial marking for any question.
- **Mode:** Computer Based Test (CBT) in English, with an on-screen virtual scientific calculator.

## Syllabus

The official EE syllabus consists of 10 sections:

1. **Engineering Mathematics:** Linear Algebra, Calculus, Differential Equations, Complex Variables, Probability and Statistics.
2. **Electric Circuits:** Network elements, KCL, KVL, nodal and mesh analysis, network theorems (Thevenin's, Norton's, Superposition, Maximum Power Transfer), transient response, sinusoidal steady-state analysis, resonance, two-port networks, balanced three-phase circuits, star-delta transformation, complex power and power factor.
3. **Electromagnetic Fields:** Coulomb's Law, Electric Field Intensity, Electric Flux Density, Gauss's Law, Divergence, field and potential of point, line, plane and spherical charge distributions, dielectrics, capacitance, Biot-Savart's law, Ampere's law, curl, Faraday's law, Lorentz force, inductance, magnetic circuits, self and mutual inductance.
4. **Signals and Systems:** Continuous and discrete time signals, shifting and scaling, LTI and causal systems, Fourier series, sampling theorem, Fourier Transform applications, Laplace and Z-transforms, RMS and average values of periodic waveforms.
5. **Electrical Machines:** Single-phase transformers (equivalent circuit, phasor diagram, tests, regulation, efficiency), three-phase transformers (connections, vector groups, parallel operation), auto-transformers, electromechanical energy conversion, DC machines (separately excited, series, shunt, characteristics, speed control), three-phase induction machines (principles, types, performance, torque-speed, tests, equivalent circuit, starting, speed control), single-phase induction motors, synchronous machines (cylindrical and salient pole, regulation, parallel operation, starting), losses and efficiency.
6. **Power Systems:** Generation concepts, AC/DC transmission, line and cable models, economic load dispatch, series/shunt compensation, insulators, distribution systems, per-unit quantities, bus admittance matrix, load flow (Gauss-Seidel, Newton-Raphson), voltage and frequency control, power factor correction, symmetrical components, fault analysis, protection (over-current, differential, directional, distance), circuit breakers, stability, equal area criterion.
7. **Control Systems:** Mathematical modeling, feedback principle, transfer function, block diagrams, signal flow graphs, transient and steady-state analysis, stability (Routh-Hurwitz, Nyquist), Bode plots, root loci, compensators, PID controllers, state space models.
8. **Electrical and Electronic Measurements:** Bridges and potentiometers, measurement of voltage, current, power, energy, power factor, instrument transformers, DMMs, phase/time/frequency measurement, oscilloscopes, error analysis.
9. **Analog and Digital Electronics:** Diode circuits (clipping, clamping, rectifiers), amplifiers, oscillators, feedback amplifiers, operational amplifiers (characteristics, applications), active filters, VCOs, timers, combinatorial and sequential logic, multiplexers, demultiplexers, Schmitt triggers, sample and hold, ADCs and DACs.
10. **Power Electronics:** Thyristor, MOSFET, IGBT characteristics and firing/gating, DC-DC converters (buck, boost, buck-boost), uncontrolled rectifiers, commutated converters, AC-DC voltage source converters, harmonics, power factor, inverters (single and three-phase), SPWM.

For detailed topics, refer to the [official GATE EE syllabus PDF](https://gate2027.iitm.ac.in/static/doc/GATE2027_Syllabus/EE_GATE2027_Syllabus.pdf).

## How to prepare (free, in order)

1. **Foundations First:** Master Engineering Mathematics and Electric Circuits thoroughly, as they form the bedrock for electrical machines, power systems, and control systems.
2. **Core Technical Mastery:** Study Electrical Machines, Power Systems, Control Systems, and Power Electronics alongside Signals & Systems and Measurements.
3. **Practice Past Papers:** Solve official GATE EE past papers topic-by-topic from the official GATE portal using the virtual scientific calculator.
