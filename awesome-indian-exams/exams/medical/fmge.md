# FMGE (Foreign Medical Graduate Examination)

FMGE is the screening test that Indian citizens and Overseas Citizens of India with a foreign medical
degree must pass to register as doctors in India. NBEMS now holds it three times a year. The October
2026 session is on 31 October 2026.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Conducted by | National Board of Examinations in Medical Sciences (NBEMS) |
| Who can appear | Indian citizen / OCI with a foreign primary medical qualification recognised for practice in the awarding country; final result declared by the bulletin's cut-off date; Eligibility Certificate (if applicable) plus degree/provisional pass certificate required |
| Total | 300 multiple-choice questions, 300 marks, one paper in two parts on a single day |
| Time | 150 questions in 150 minutes per part (+ up to 3 minutes attendance time): Part I 09:00–11:30, Part II 14:00–16:30 |
| Negative marking | None |
| Pass mark | Minimum 150 of 300; result declared Pass / Fail (no re-totaling, re-evaluation or grace marks) |
| October 2026 dates | Admit card 27 October 2026 (download only) · exam 31 October 2026 · result by 30 November 2026 |

## Exam pattern

One computer-based paper of 300 MCQs in English only (4 options each), split into Part I and Part II of
150 questions on the same day with a break in between. Each part is divided into time-bound sections
(for example 3 sections of 50 questions in 50 minutes each): you cannot move ahead early or return to a
section after its time ends. One mark per question, no negative marking. A minimum of 150 of 300
qualifies you; Part I and Part II marks are not published separately. There is no cap on attempts, but
once you qualify you cannot sit FMGE again.

## Syllabus

The syllabus follows the Competency-Based Undergraduate Curriculum published by the National Medical
Commission for the MBBS course. The subject-wise blueprint (300 marks total):

Pre and para-clinical subjects, 100 marks: Anatomy 17 · Physiology 17 · Biochemistry 17 ·
Pathology 13 · Microbiology 13 · Pharmacology 13 · Forensic Medicine 10.

Clinical subjects, 200 marks: Medicine 33 · General Surgery 32 · Obstetrics & Gynaecology 30 ·
Community Medicine 30 · Pediatrics 15 · Ophthalmology 15 · Otorhinolaryngology 15 ·
Psychiatry 5 · Dermatology & STD 5 · Radiotherapy 5 · Anesthesiology 5 · Orthopedics 5 ·
Radiodiagnosis 5.

## How to prepare (free, in order)

1. Pass mark, not rank, is the target: secure the 150 with full coverage rather than deep peaks.
2. Clinical subjects carry two-thirds of the marks; Medicine, Surgery, OBG and Community Medicine alone are nearly half the paper.
3. Practise in sealed 50-minute blocks with no going back, matching the time-bound sections.
