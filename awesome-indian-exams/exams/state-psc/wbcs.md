## Exam pattern

**Preliminary:** one paper on General Studies — objective, 200 multiple-choice questions,
200 marks, 2½ hours, graduate standard. The 200 marks split into eight fields of 25 marks each:
English Composition; General Science; Current events of National and International Importance;
History of India; Geography of India with special reference to West Bengal; Indian Polity and
Economy; Indian National Movement; General Mental Ability. It is a screening test only: the marks
do not count for final selection, and only prelim-qualified candidates are admitted to that year's Main.

**Main:** six compulsory papers of 200 marks each (3 hours each), plus — only for Group A and/or B
candidates — one optional subject of two papers (200 marks each). Four compulsory papers
(General Studies-I, General Studies-II, Constitution of India and Indian Economy, Arithmetic and
Test of Reasoning) are MCQ type answered on OMR sheets; Paper I (language) and Paper II (English)
are conventional written papers. There is negative marking for wrong MCQ answers (the scheme
document does not print the per-question deduction — take it from the detailed advertisement).
The Commission may fix qualifying marks paper-wise and in aggregate; a paper without qualifying
marks is not counted in the aggregate.

| Paper | Marks |
|---|---:|
| Paper I: Bengali / Hindi / Urdu / Nepali / Santali (letter/report, précis, composition, translation from English) | 200 |
| Paper II: English (letter/report, précis, composition, translation) | 200 |
| Paper III: General Studies-I | 200 |
| Paper IV: General Studies-II | 200 |
| Paper V: Constitution of India and Indian Economy (incl. RBI role and functions) | 200 |
| Paper VI: Arithmetic and Test of Reasoning | 200 |
| Optional subject, two papers (Groups A and B only) | 2 × 200 |

**Personality Test:** one test per candidate (no separate test per group): 200 marks for Group A,
200 for Group B, 150 for Group C, 100 for Group D. Totals: 1800 (A), 1800 (B), 1350 (C), 1300 (D).

## Syllabus

- **Preliminary:** the eight fields above; the scheme's Appendix I outlines each (for example
  English Composition covers synonyms, antonyms, idioms, phrasal verbs and vocabulary).
- **Main GS-I:** Indian History with emphasis on the National Movement; Geography of India with
  special reference to West Bengal.
- **Main GS-II:** Science and scientific/technological advancement, Environment, General Knowledge
  and Current Affairs.
- **Main Paper V:** the Constitution of India and the Indian Economy, including the role and
  functions of the Reserve Bank of India.
- **Main Paper VI:** Arithmetic at about Madhyamik (Class 10, West Bengal Board) standard, plus
  Test of Reasoning (analytical and logical reasoning, series, data inference, non-verbal reasoning).
- **Optional subjects (38):** literature papers (Bengali, Hindi, Sanskrit, English, Pali, Arabic,
  Persian, French, Urdu, Santali, Nepali, Comparative Literature) and Agriculture, Animal Husbandry
  and Veterinary Science, Anthropology, Botany, Chemistry, Civil / Electrical / Mechanical
  Engineering, Commerce and Accountancy, Computer Science, Economics, Geography, Geology, History,
  Law, Mathematics, Management, Medical Science, Philosophy, Physiology, Physics, Political Science,
  Psychology, Sociology, Statistics, Zoology.
- The scheme notes the syllabi are indicative and questions may assume the degree-level
  qualification prescribed in the advertisement.

## How to prepare (free, in order)

1. Cover the shared UPSC-style GS base once (polity, history, geography, economy) — it carries
   both the prelim and the Main GS papers (see the [overlap map](../../resources/overlap-map.md)).
2. Add West Bengal specifics: state history and the National Movement, West Bengal geography,
   state economy and schemes, from official state sources.
3. Practise Madhyamik-level arithmetic plus reasoning daily — Paper VI is MCQ on an OMR sheet
   with negative marking, so speed with accuracy matters.
4. Write daily: précis, letter/report and translation for the two compulsory language papers.
5. Solve the Commission's own papers and answer keys from psc.wb.gov.in for the real question style.
