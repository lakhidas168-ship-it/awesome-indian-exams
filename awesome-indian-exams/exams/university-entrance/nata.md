# NATA (National Aptitude Test in Architecture)

NATA is the aptitude test for B.Arch admission, conducted by the Council of Architecture since 2006.
In 2026 it is held in two phases: Phase 1 on Fridays and Saturdays from 4 April to 13 June 2026, and
Phase 2 on 7 and 8 August 2026. A candidate may appear in only one phase.

## Exam pattern

**Part A — Drawing and Composition Test (offline, 90 minutes, 80 marks, 3 questions):**

- A1: Composition and Colour, 1 question, 25 marks — composing and colouring arrangements of shapes.
- A2: Sketching and Composition (black and white), 1 question, 25 marks — drawing buildings, people, environment and products with scale, proportion, texture, shade and shadow.
- A3: 3D Composition, 1 question, 30 marks — building a 3D composition for a given situation using the kit provided at the centre.

**Part B — MCQ and NCQ (computer-based adaptive test, 90 minutes, 120 marks, 50 questions):**

- B1: 42 multiple-choice questions.
- B2: 8 no-choice questions.

Sessions run 10.00 am–1.00 pm (morning) and 1.30 pm–4.30 pm (afternoon). Phase 1 uses
percentile-based scoring: each attempt generates a raw score, and after all Phase 1 sessions the
final scorecard carries the best raw score with a percentile score computed against all examinees.
Phase 2 generates raw scores only. There is no minimum raw score prescribed; the final scorecard
with the best raw score and a non-zero percentile score is the valid qualifying score. Candidates
who attempted Phase 1 cannot appear in Phase 2.

## Syllabus

Part A: composition and colour, sketching and visualisation, 3D composition as described above.

Part B draws on some or all of the following: visual reasoning (2D/3D composition and technical
concepts); logical derivation (decoding situations and drawing conclusions); general knowledge of
architecture and design (important buildings, historical progression, materials and construction
technology, current issues); language interpretation (English vocabulary, sentences and grammar);
design sensitivity and thinking (observation, analysis, critical thinking, problem identification);
numerical ability (basic mathematics with geometry and creative thinking).

## How to prepare (free, in order)

1. Practise Part A drawing daily: quick compositions with dry colours (A1), pencil sketching of buildings and people with shade and shadow (A2), and small 3D models from household material (A3).
2. Work through NCERT Class 11–12 mathematics for the numerical ability and geometry in Part B.
3. Build visual reasoning and design sensitivity: observe buildings around you, note materials and structure, and read about important buildings and movements in architecture.
4. Practise timed mixed MCQ sets covering logical reasoning, English comprehension and general awareness of architecture and design.
