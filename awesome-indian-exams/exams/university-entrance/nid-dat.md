# NID DAT

The Design Aptitude Test (DAT) is the entrance examination for admission to the Bachelor of Design (B.Des.) / 5.5-Year Integrated M.Des. and Master of Design (M.Des.) programmes at the National Institute of Design (NID) across its campuses at Ahmedabad, Gandhinagar, Bengaluru, Andhra Pradesh, Assam, Haryana, and Madhya Pradesh.

## Exam pattern

The admission process consists of two stages, common across all participating NIDs:

### Stage 1: DAT Prelims
- **Format**: Paper-and-pencil/pen test conducted simultaneously at all centres.
- **Duration**: Announced on the admit card.
- **Question type**: Questions may involve text and visuals.
- **Medium**: English only.
- **Common paper**: Single test paper for B.Des./Integrated M.Des.; single common test paper for all M.Des. disciplines.
- **Scoring**: Used **only** for shortlisting to DAT Mains. Prelims marks are **not carried forward** to the final merit.

### Stage 2: DAT Mains
Shortlisted candidates (2.5× seats per category) are invited.

**For B.Des. / 5.5-Year Integrated M.Des.**  
DAT Mains consists of two parts:
1. **Studio Sensitivity Test** — hands-on tasks assessing material handling, visualisation, and creative problem-solving.
2. **In-Person Sensitivity Test** — evaluation of communication, observation, and behavioural abilities.

**For M.Des. (including Design Education)**  
DAT Mains is conducted separately by each NID for its disciplines:
- NID Ahmedabad/Gandhinagar/Bengaluru: Studio Test (group-wise, except Group X which is discipline-wise) + separate Interview per discipline.
- NID Madhya Pradesh & NID Haryana: Separate schedule, format, and venue announced on respective institute websites.

**Weightage for final merit**: Based on DAT Mains scores only; weightages announced later. Only candidates who appear for **all** Mains components are considered for the final merit list.

> **Note**: In unforeseen circumstances (e.g., pandemic), NID reserves the right to conduct all tests entirely online, entirely offline, or in a hybrid mode. Check the admissions website for updates.

## Syllabus

NID does **not** publish a detailed topic-wise syllabus. The handbooks describe the broad competencies assessed:

- **DAT Prelims**: Design awareness, visual sensitivity, observation, drawing/sketching skills, analytical and logical reasoning, creativity, and problem-solving through text-and-visual questions.
- **DAT Mains (B.Des.)**: Studio Sensitivity Test (hands-on material exploration, 3D visualisation, model-making, thematic composition) and In-Person Sensitivity Test (portfolio discussion, communication, behavioural assessment).
- **DAT Mains (M.Des.)**: Studio Test (discipline-specific creative tasks) and Interview (portfolio review, domain knowledge, design thinking, motivation).

Candidates are advised to develop core design aptitude — sketching, visual thinking, observation, and creative problem-solving — rather than prepare for fixed topics.

## How to prepare (free, in order)

1. **Read the current handbook end-to-end** — eligibility, dates, test centres, reservation, fees, and exam structure. The 2027–28 handbooks are linked above.
2. **Build core design aptitude daily** — timed sketching from life, visual memory exercises, material exploration (paper, clay, wire), and creative problem-solving prompts.
3. **Practise past-year questions from memory-based collections** — since NID doesn't release papers, aspirant forums and coaching blogs often compile memory-based questions; use them only as practice material, not as official sources.
4. **Prepare a portfolio** (for Mains) — curate 10–15 pieces showing range: sketches, 3D models, digital work, process documentation. Be ready to discuss each piece.
5. **Track amendments** — NID publishes amendments on the admissions website (e.g., seat changes, edit-window dates). Check weekly until the exam.
6. **Plan logistics early** — test centre allotment is not guaranteed; arrange travel/accommodation for both Prelims and Mains independently.
