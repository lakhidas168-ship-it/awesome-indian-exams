# RRB Group D (Level 1)

> **Evidence status: 🟢 official.** Every number below is from CEN No. 09/2025 (Level-1 posts of the
> 7th CPC pay matrix), Corrigendum No. 5, and the revised CBT schedule notice, fetched from
> rrbsecunderabad.gov.in on 2026-09-28.

RRB Group D (Level 1) recruits Track Maintainer-IV, Pointsman-B and Assistant posts (electrical,
S&T, track machine, bridge, carriage and wagon, loco shed, P.Way and others) across Indian
Railways. It is Class 10/ITI level and is usually the single largest recruitment exam in the country.

| | |

| Minimum CBT marks | 40% (UR/EWS), 30% (OBC-NCL/SC/ST); relaxable by 2 marks for PwBD against reserved vacancies |

## Exam pattern

**CBT:** 100 questions, 100 marks (1 mark each), 90 minutes (120 minutes for scribe-eligible
candidates), **−1/3** per wrong answer. The section split below is indicative and may vary slightly
in the actual paper.

| Section | Questions |
|---|---:|
| General Intelligence and Reasoning | 30 |
| Mathematics | 25 |
| General Science | 25 |
| General Awareness and Current Affairs | 20 |

Shortlisting for PET is 3× the community-wise vacancies on CBT merit (with normalisation across
shifts); shortlisting for document verification is 1:1 on CBT merit plus PET qualification. The
Railway reserves the right to hold the CBT in single or multi-stage mode.

**Physical Efficiency Test (qualifying):** men must lift and carry 35 kg for 100 metres in 2 minutes
and run 1,000 metres in 4 minutes 15 seconds; women must lift and carry 20 kg for 100 metres in
2 minutes and run 1,000 metres in 5 minutes 40 seconds — each in one chance. The weight-carrying
test comes first and eliminates. PwBD candidates, ex-servicemen and Course Completed Act
Apprentices are exempt from PET.

## Syllabus

Class 10 level, per the CEN (topics illustrative, not exhaustive): NCERT physics, chemistry and life
science; arithmetic and elementary maths (number system, BODMAS, decimals, fractions, LCM/HCF,
ratio and proportion, percentages, mensuration, time and work, time and distance, simple and
compound interest, profit and loss, algebra, geometry and trigonometry, elementary statistics,
square roots, age calculations, calendar and clock, pipes and cisterns); verbal and non-verbal
reasoning (analogies, series, coding-decoding, syllogism, Venn diagrams, data interpretation and
sufficiency, directions, statement-arguments and assumptions); current affairs and static GK
(science and technology, sports, culture, personalities, economics, politics).

## How to prepare (free, in order)

1. General Science is a quarter of the paper and comes almost entirely from NCERT Class 6–10. Read those books first.
2. Start physical training at the same time as the written preparation.
3. Practise full papers at 90 minutes. The −1/3 penalty makes blind guessing costly.
