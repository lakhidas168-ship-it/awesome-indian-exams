# UPSC Civil Services Examination (CSE) for engineers

> **Evidence status: 🟢 official.** Scheme, marks, paper formats, and the Electrical Engineering optional
> topic list below were checked on 2026-10-04 against the UPSC *Civil Services (Preliminary) Examination, 2026*
> notification, the *Civil Services Examination Rules – 2026* (Gazette of India Extraordinary, via DoPT CSE portal),
> and official CSM General Studies question papers on `upsc.gov.in`, all opened this run.

Many engineers take CSE. This page covers the examination structure, the **General Studies Mains answer-writing**
framework, and the **Electrical Engineering optional**, which overlaps heavily with what a GATE/ESE EE aspirant
already studies.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Conducted by | Union Public Service Commission (UPSC) |
| Stages | Preliminary (objective, screening) → Main (descriptive written) → Personality test |
| Final merit | Mains written 1750 + Personality test 275 = **2025**. Prelims only decides who reaches Mains |
| Age and attempts | Set by the notification (general category: 21 to 32 years on 1 August 2026, 6 attempts, with category relaxations) |
| Qualification | Any graduate degree from a recognised university |

## Exam pattern

**Prelims** (negative marking: one-third of the question's marks deducted per wrong answer; blank answers carry no penalty)

| Paper | Marks | Time | Role |
|---|---:|---|---|
| General Studies I | 200 | 2 h | Decides who qualifies for Mains |
| General Studies II (CSAT) | 200 | 2 h | Qualifying, minimum 33% (66 marks) needed |

**Mains** (descriptive written; each paper is of three hours)

| Paper | Marks | Counted in merit |
|---|---:|---|
| Paper A: Indian language (from 8th Schedule) | 300 | No (qualifying, 25% minimum) |
| Paper B: English | 300 | No (qualifying, 25% minimum) |
| Paper I: Essay | 250 | Yes |
| Paper II: General Studies I | 250 | Yes |
| Paper III: General Studies II | 250 | Yes |
| Paper IV: General Studies III | 250 | Yes |
| Paper V: General Studies IV | 250 | Yes |
| Paper VI: Optional Subject Paper 1 | 250 | Yes |
| Paper VII: Optional Subject Paper 2 | 250 | Yes |

Essay, General Studies and optional papers are evaluated only for candidates who score at least 25% (75/300 marks)
in each of qualifying Papers A and B.

**General Studies question paper format** (from official CSM papers):
Each General Studies paper (Papers II to V) carries 250 marks with 20 compulsory questions in 3 hours (180 minutes):
- Questions 1–10: 10 marks each, 150-word limit (~7 minutes per question).
- Questions 11–20: 15 marks each, 250-word limit (~11 minutes per question).
- Target pace: 7–8 minutes for a 10-marker, 11–12 minutes for a 15-marker, reserving 10 minutes for reading and review.

## Syllabus

The official notification details the syllabi for all nine Mains papers.

**General Studies papers overview:**
- **General Studies I (Paper II):** Indian Heritage and Culture, History and Geography of the World and Society.
- **General Studies II (Paper III):** Governance, Constitution, Polity, Social Justice and International relations.
- **General Studies III (Paper IV):** Technology, Economic Development, Bio-diversity, Environment, Security and Disaster Management.
- **General Studies IV (Paper V):** Ethics, Integrity and Aptitude.

## How to prepare (free, in order)

1. **Build the General Studies foundation first.** Prelims GS-I (200 marks) decides who qualifies for Mains, while
   CSAT requires 33%. Study NCERT textbooks (Classes 6–12) for history, geography, polity, economics and general science.
   Read the Press Information Bureau (PIB) daily for government schemes and policy announcements.
2. **Mains carries the merit.** Essay plus the four General Studies papers make up 1250 of the 1750 written marks,
   so begin weekly answer writing early against the syllabus in the notification.
3. **Decode the directive words (from inv:1469c0f5).** Mains questions demand precise treatment based on their directive:
   - *Critically examine*: Evaluate both sides against evidence — where the policy or institution succeeded and where it
     stalled or produced unintended stress — and deliver a reasoned, balanced verdict (do not offer one-sided advocacy).
   - *Discuss*: Address different facets of the debate, outline contrasting viewpoints, and explore implications.
   - *Comment*: State a clear, reasoned viewpoint directly and substantiate it with verifiable facts.
   - *Analyse*: Break the topic into constitutive components (root causes, structural bottlenecks, stakeholder incentives)
     and explain how they interact.
4. **Structure answers across multiple dimensions.** Split the demand into clear subheadings. Balance relevant
   dimensions (administrative, economic, constitutional/legal, technological, environmental, and ethical). Ground claims
   strictly in official data (Union Budget, Economic Survey, NITI Aayog, Law Commission reports, RBI data, constitutional
   articles, Supreme Court judgments) rather than unsubstantiated claims.
5. **Use free AI prompts for rubric-based self-evaluation.** Write answers timed (7 minutes for a 10-marker, 11 minutes
   for a 15-marker). Paste your draft into the [UPSC Mains answer evaluator](../../tools/ai-study-prompts.md#2-upsc-mains-answer-evaluator)
   prompt to audit demand fulfillment, introductory brevity, dimensional balance, substantiation validity, and word limit
   discipline. See an authentic syllabus-grounded breakdown in [Mains practice breakdown](../../resources/mains-practice/2026-09-28.md).
6. **Choose and complete the optional subject early.** For an engineer, the Electrical Engineering optional reuses the
   GATE/ESE core. Focus on bridging the non-GATE topics (analog and digital communication, microprocessors) early.
7. **Practise on the official papers under exam time.** UPSC publishes past question papers free, including EE optional
   and General Studies papers. Solve them under strict 3-hour exam conditions to build endurance and time management.
8. **Clear the qualifying language papers.** Papers A and B need 25% each (75/300 marks) and are not counted in merit.
   Review past papers to verify matriculation-level language competency.
