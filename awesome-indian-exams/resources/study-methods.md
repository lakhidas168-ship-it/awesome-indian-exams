# How to study: methods that work, for free

Coaching sells schedules, tests and motivation. The methods underneath are free, and learning research has tested
them for decades. This page puts the evidence together with what toppers and aspirants repeat on forums, and links
each method to a free tool in this list.

> **Evidence status.** The first section cites peer-reviewed research. The tips after it are common aspirant advice
> gathered on 2026-09-27: they are sensible, but they are experience, not proof.

## The two methods with the strongest evidence

A large review of ten popular study techniques rated only two as "high utility" for learners of all ages and
abilities: **practice testing** and **distributed practice**. It rated rereading and highlighting as low utility,
even though they feel productive
([Dunlosky et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266)).

1. **Test yourself instead of rereading.** Students who took recall tests on a passage remembered much more a week
   later than students who reread it the same number of times
   ([Roediger and Karpicke, 2006](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x)). For exams this means:
   previous-year questions, flashcards and mocks *are* the studying, not just a check at the end.
   → [Flashcards](../tools/flashcards.md) · [Previous papers](previous-papers.md)
2. **Space it out.** The same hours spread over days and weeks beat one long session. The longer you need to
   remember something, the bigger the gaps should be
   ([Cepeda et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354)). For a long exam cycle, revise each module
   again after a few days, a few weeks and a few months.
   → [Flashcards](../tools/flashcards.md) bring each card back at growing gaps automatically.

The same review rated **interleaving** (mixing topics within one practice session) and **self-explanation**
(explaining each step to yourself) as moderately useful. Mixed-topic mocks are interleaving by design.

## A free routine built on them

| When | What | Tool |
|---|---|---|

## Aspirant tips that come up again and again

**Previous papers first, always.**

- Previous-year questions show which topics really come back. SSC and state commissions in particular repeat
  question patterns. Tag every PYQ with its module and topic, then prepare the most-asked topics first.
- Solve one previous paper a week. Do not start the next one until you have understood every mistake in the last.

**Mocks are for analysis, not for scores.**

- A mock without analysis is wasted. Spend as long analysing it as you spent taking it.
- Sort every mistake into one of four kinds: concept gap, silly mistake, time pressure, or guess. Fix concept gaps
  with study, silly mistakes with a checklist, time pressure with sectional drills, and guesses with the
  negative-marking maths in the [marks calculator](../tools/score-calculator.md).
- Take mocks at the same time of day as the real exam. Use the official interface where one exists:
  NTA Abhyas and SATHEE are free ([free coaching](free-coaching.md)).

**Fewer sources, more revisions.**

- One good source per topic, revised three times, beats five sources read once. Start with NCERT and the official
  syllabus.
- Make short notes only for what you keep forgetting. Better still, make them flashcards.

**Plan backwards from the exam date.**

- Take the date from the official notification, keep the last weeks for mocks, and give weak modules the earliest
  slots. The [study planner](../tools/study-planner.md) does this for you.
- Most exams share most of their syllabus. Prepare shared modules once and aim at two or three exams of the same
  family: see the [overlap map](overlap-map.md).

**Protect the basics.**

- Sleep, exercise and one lighter day a week are part of the plan, not a break from it. Long exam cycles are lost
  to burnout more often than to missing topics.
- Study in focused blocks (for example 25 or 50 minutes) with short breaks. The
  [focus timer](../tools/study-planner.md) keeps a daily streak.

**Stay on the right side of the rules.**

- Download papers only from official sites, and books only from official or openly licensed sources. Pirated PDF
  channels are illegal and often carry malware.
- Never pay an agent to "get" a free scheme or a result. Official portals only.
