## Today's question (GS Paper III, 10 marks, 150 words)

**Syllabus topic:** General Studies-III — *"Investment models"* (CSE 2026 notification,
Appendix I, Mains syllabus; also draws on *"Infrastructure: Energy, Ports, Roads, Airports,
Railways etc."*).

**Question:** *Private capital is often presented as the answer to India's infrastructure
financing gap, yet several public-private partnership projects have stalled in renegotiation
or dispute. Critically examine the investment models available for infrastructure creation
in India. (150 words)*

Format follows the official paper: GS papers carry 250 marks each with twenty compulsory
questions — Questions 1–10 in 150 words, Questions 11–20 in 250 words
(CSM-2026 GS Paper III, instructions page).

## Directive word: critically examine

*Critically examine* asks for more than a description. It wants a judgment built in front of
the examiner:

1. Lay out the thing asked (here: the models — budgetary support, PPP variants such as
   BOT, HAM and TOT, asset monetisation/recycling, institutional finance).
2. Test each against evidence — where it worked, where it stalled, and why (risk sharing,
   dispute resolution, financing cost, execution capacity).
3. End with a balanced verdict: which model fits which situation, not a one-sided conclusion.

Contrast: *discuss* only debates the points; *comment* gives your view briefly;
*analyse* breaks the topic into parts and shows how they connect. None of them demands the
for-and-against verdict that *critically examine* does.

## How to approach it (not a model answer)

- Split the demand: (a) list the models with one identifying feature each; (b) examine each
  critically with one success and one failure/stress point; (c) close with a suitability
  verdict (2–3 lines).
- Cover at least three dimensions: financing/fiscal, execution/risk, and institutional
  (contracts, regulation, dispute resolution).
- Substantiate from official sources only: the Union Budget, the Economic Survey, NITI Aayog
  or RBI documents — never invent figures, committee names or project data.
- Common pitfalls: writing only about PPPs and ignoring other models; giving features
  without criticism; ending without a verdict after a *critically examine* demand.

## Get your answer evaluated (free)

1. Write your answer in 150 words, timed (about 7 minutes, the Mains pace for a 10-marker).
2. Open the [UPSC Mains answer evaluator](../../tools/ai-study-prompts.md) prompt, paste
   the question and your answer into any free AI chat, and follow its rubric (demand of the
   question, introduction, body, substantiation, conclusion, word limit).
3. The prompt rewrites only your weakest paragraph — revise from that, and keep an error
   log of repeated mistakes.
