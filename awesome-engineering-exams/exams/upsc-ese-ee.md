# UPSC Engineering Services Examination (ESE / IES): Electrical Engineering

> **Evidence status: 🟡 secondary.** The pattern below has been stable for several cycles. On 2026-09-27 it
> was cross-checked against multiple non-official sources. Checking it against the ESE 2027 notification on
> upsc.gov.in is hive task `T-002`.

ESE recruits Group A and B engineers for central government engineering services. For an EE graduate it is
the hardest and most rewarding of these exams. Its technical syllabus is GATE EE plus a few extra subjects, and
it adds a general studies paper and descriptive mains papers.

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Conducted by | Union Public Service Commission (UPSC) |
| Stages | Preliminary (objective) → Main (descriptive) → Personality test |
| Final merit | Prelims 500 + Mains 600 + Personality test 200 = **1300**. Prelims marks count towards final merit |
| Age | 21 to 30 years, with category relaxations as per the notification |
| Qualification | Degree in engineering, or an equivalent listed in the notification |
| Services | The notification lists the services and posts for each engineering discipline every year |

## Exam pattern

| Stage | Paper | Type | Marks | Time |
|---|---|---|---:|---|
| Prelims | Paper I: General Studies and Engineering Aptitude (common to all disciplines) | Objective | 200 | 2 h |
| Prelims | Paper II: Electrical Engineering | Objective | 300 | 3 h |
| Mains | Paper I: Electrical Engineering | Descriptive | 300 | 3 h |
| Mains | Paper II: Electrical Engineering | Descriptive | 300 | 3 h |
| Personality test | Interview | — | 200 | — |

Objective papers carry a **negative mark of one-third** of the question's marks for each wrong answer.

## Syllabus

**Prelims Paper I (General Studies and Engineering Aptitude)** covers ten areas:
current issues of national and international importance relating to social, economic and industrial
development; engineering aptitude (logical reasoning and analytical ability); engineering mathematics and
numerical analysis; general principles of design, drawing and safety; standards and quality practices in
production, construction, maintenance and services; basics of energy and environment (conservation, pollution,
climate change, environmental impact assessment); basics of project management; basics of material science
and engineering; ICT-based tools and their applications in engineering (networking, e-governance,
technology-based education); ethics and values in the engineering profession.

**Electrical Engineering** (Prelims Paper II covers all twelve; Mains splits them in two):

| Mains Paper I | Mains Paper II |
|---|---|
| Engineering Mathematics | Analog and Digital Electronics |
| Electrical Materials | Systems and Signal Processing |
| Electric Circuits and Fields | Control Systems |
| Electrical and Electronic Measurements | Electrical Machines |
| Computer Fundamentals | Power Systems |
| Basic Electronics Engineering | Power Electronics and Drives |

Compared with GATE EE, the extra technical subjects are **Electrical Materials** and **Computer
Fundamentals**, plus microprocessor basics inside Analog and Digital Electronics. See the
[subject map](../resources/subject-map.md).

## How to prepare (free, in order)

1. Build the technical core on the [GATE EE](gate-ee.md) sequence. It covers about 10 of the 12 ESE subjects.
2. Add **Electrical Materials** and **Computer Fundamentals** once the core is stable.
3. Treat **Paper I** as a separate subject, 30 to 45 minutes a day from the start. Its official sources
   (PIB, Ministry of Power, MNRE, BEE, CEA) are free and listed in the resources page.
4. For Mains, practise **descriptive answers** from the official papers: derivation, labelled diagram, units, and
   a final line that states the result. Write, don't just read.
5. Solve every official Prelims paper topic-wise, then as timed full papers.
